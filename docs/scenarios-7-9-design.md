# Scenarios 7 to 9: The Exile, The Pale Court, The Drowned Abbey

Part of the scenario design; index in scenarios-design.md. Siblings: scenarios-1-3-design.md, scenarios-4-6-design.md, scenarios-10-11-design.md; the engine's scenario machinery is in scenario-system-design.md. Numbers, waves, texts and tile coordinates live in `scripts/scenarios/scenario_<n>.gd` (the source of truth); this page says why. Cutscene wording is not copied here.

The three scenarios share a thread: **earning trust or breaking an enemy's hold with no safety net** (no revive from 7 on in the sense that matters: Brenna is away from home). 7 is about being a guest, 8 about refusing Rhodri's offer, 9 about a village that must see what she is before it gives up its relic.

## Scenario 7: The Exile

**Story.** Fiona's refuge, a hidden forest valley: refugees, court mages, the druids' grove by the stone circle, a mage's tower, Pell's wagon. The court mages are finishing a working that hides the valley. Brenna comes because of what was done to her company; Fiona has reason to distrust a knight of Rhodri's army and rejects her. A parallel is intended: two women rejected by the same side, and Fiona now rejects Brenna for having served it. Fiona has learned the crown binds people, and Brenna has nowhere else to go, so the refuge is where the two have to settle it. Fiona does not fight and must not die; her hut falling is the usual rule (the villager of a lost hut dies), so no special case. Monsters (Wilderness, Undead, some Renegades, a few witches) keep coming because the refuge is magical.

**Beats.**
- Intro: Brenna and Pell come out of the trees; Fiona waits at the gate with two mages.
- After wave 2 Fiona opens up a little. After wave 7 she permits new buildings and tells Brenna why their father never named an heir (`story-design.md` §4, *Why Orsin named no heir*: law and wish were hers, the army would not follow a queen, naming Rhodri would have split church and mages from the crown). Her closing line is that he was waiting for Brenna's side to change its mind, and she was his answer.
- The grove's wave (wave 5): the druids' elder calls for help and says why they stayed out of the war (`story-design.md` §4: the pact with the first king, the church once drove them from the towns). They fight for the grove, not for Fiona; the stone circle then answers (the summoner).
- Outro: a sealed scroll for the court mages; only someone of House Aldmoor can end what Rhodri has become, but Fiona cannot go yet; Brenna promises to bring her when the time comes.

**Why it is built this way.**
- **Trust as locks.** Fiona's trust is made playable by command locks, so the player feels like a guest: Brenna lives in a tent outside the walls (`Scenario.hero_lives_out`) and until the first trust step the villagers take no orders and nothing may be built (units can still be hired and stationed); the next step lets her move in and opens repairs, rebuilding and villager orders; the last opens new buildings. What is locked is named command by command (building, repairs, hut rebuilds, upgrades, tear-down, recruiting, retiring or assigning villagers, restoring ruins), so the guest feeling holds whichever way the player tries. The locks are `Scenario.command_error`, asked after the runner's own checks. The mage's tower waits for the first step, the stone circle for the grove's wave, so each unit unlock is also a story reward.
- **The druids take no orders.** Three named druid summoners sit on the benches of a **grove** (a campaign barracks by the stone circle) and fight as barracks units do, but serve their grove, not the player. A druid downed during the grove's wave is a defeat (one still down from before counts once it stood again); otherwise they revive as usual. This gives a protect-the-NPC objective without making the player micromanage them. `Commands` refuses moving, withdrawing, swapping, upgrading and sending a bound unit (the one-place-for-orders rule applied), and the grove has no upgrades or tear-down. The band that threatens it comes down the north road past the grove. Anselm vouches for Brenna at the gate, but she still wears Rhodri's colours.
- The farm sits by the south road only because the forest leaves no room at the walls.

**What it added to the engine, and why** (details in scenario-system-design.md). Residents who stay home and walk rounds, for Fiona and the priests. `MilitaryUnit.bound` plus `Grove` for units that refuse orders; only druids serve in a grove (`military-design.md` §3b, §6). Unlock sites that a scenario awakens on its own schedule (`Scenario.sites`, `ScenarioRunner.awaken_site`, `Scenario.unlocked`), and `"near_site"` to put a prebuilt building next to a site. The hero living out of the village (`Hero.home_override`, `ScenarioRunner.hero_moves_in`). Scenario-specific command rules (`Scenario.command_error`).

## Scenario 8: The Pale Court

**Story.** The outskirts of the burned capital, a ruined court district on ash land near a volcano. Brenna has no army, only a band that came with her from the refuge (court mages, acolytes, a few archers and volunteers). The goal is to clear **three crypts** under the court. The monsters are Evil (vampires, necromancers, undead, gargoyles as the palace's stone guardians, witches) and the **Dread Legion**, which holds the court's gates in camps; only the herald speaks.

**Beats.**
- Intro: the capital by night, the palace dark against a red sky; Lothar, herald of the Pale Court, waits under a white flag and bows to Brenna. He is the court's one recurring herald and **survives** until scenarios 10 and 11 need him.
- Lothar carries Rhodri's offer: come back to his side. Accepting loses the scenario.
- At the last crypt the champion **Rurik** appears (the first mid-boss, as the README roadmap wanted).
- Outro: the throne hall's gate stands open; Brenna sees that the crown is not a gift but feeds on him (`story-design.md` §4: the old king's crown, reworked by a foreign goldsmith). A church survivor says some priests hid something that might break such a thing, the **First King's Hand**, in the abbey that sank in the delta (nobody knows whether it works); Fiona's messenger confirms. Rhodri hears she refused and laughs. The corruption is gradual and he is still partly himself.

**Why it is built this way.**
- **"No army" means no recruiting.** The band waits in the reserve and gold only levels and specialises it (`Scenario.reserve`, `Scenario.recruiting`). The point is a lesson: better, not more. The hero cannot be downed and revived, so each crypt is a real risk, while the band can still specialise.
- **Crypts are lairs the scenario wakes one by one** (the game's own lair rule, driven by `Scenario.lairs`), so pressure rises as the player clears. Awake, each is one more spawn point for the scripted waves; a cleared one stays cleared. The hero clears one as a camp. Each crypt is weaker than the next (the guards' HP grows with distance) and Rurik guards the farthest.
- **Each new Legion kind teaches a counter**: the archer shoots people in the open (never a tower's unit) from a range towers can answer, so towers and walls matter; the guardsman's tower shield blunts arrows, so mages and melee matter; the banner-bearer speeds nearby Legion soldiers, the enemy-side mirror of the warrior's war cry, so kill it first. Flyers need ranged units; vampires heal from hits. Cavalry arrives later with a hint. The waves bring the new kinds in one at a time, each with a hint when first seen, and the last wave repeats until the crypts are cleared, so the lesson sequence never runs out. Two optional **Legion camps** hold the court's gates.
- **Lothar's offer is a choice** after wave 3, so the player is asked rather than told; the losing answer exists so refusing means something.

**What it added to the engine, and why.** The four Legion kinds and Legion camps (`MonsterCamp.add_guard` for Rurik); a scenario's lairs; the band's reserve and the recruiting switch; `Treasure.sealed` and campaign items (the relic) as a reward's `"item"`; `Enemy.post` / `post_leash` and a queue entry's `"hp_x"` / `"power"` for guards and bosses; the outro waiting for a playing scene; `tools/make_scenario_map.gd` placing lairs, a treasure with its camp and an island. Map: `data/maps/pale_court.json` (Volcanic).

## Scenario 9: The Drowned Abbey

**Story.** A pilgrims' harbour village on a drowned shore; the church's old abbey stands on an island in a river delta beyond it. Brenna and Pell arrive by ferry in mist, the abbey's spires out of the water, a bell ringing under it. Two priests, the eldest and a younger one, live in the village's new church and distrust her: she is military, a knight of the army that serves Rhodri, and Fiona's word does not change what she wore. They support Fiona but feared Rhodri could rise over her (this is the priests of this place, not the church's stance).

The **First King's Hand** is a ceremonial sword of the king who founded the Wardline: a relic, not a battle weapon. The church believes it can cleanse and rule with righteousness; **nobody knows whether it helps against Rhodri**. Priests hid it and sank the abbey to keep it from him. Only someone of House Aldmoor can wield it. The Legion's captain **Lysa**, Brenna's old captain, hunts it while the main force marches home. Earning the priests' trust is the thread, as Fiona's was in 7.

**Goal.** Bring the relic from the abbey's sealed crypt home, and slay the leviathan. No number of waves: they repeat, growing, until both are done. Defeats: Pell or the eldest priest dies, Brenna is downed (no revive), or Lysa reaches the map edge with the relic. The younger priest drowning is no defeat; it is the other branch.

**Enemies.** Crabs from the shore; necromancers raising the abbey's drowned dead and vampires holding the crypt (the abbey's camp is led by the **Fallen Abbot**, a named vampire, so the camp has a face); Wilderness leftovers; the Legion column; the **leviathan** and a **guard octopus**. Three fronts run at once (the water, the bank road, the abbey), so the player must decide when to send Brenna, who cannot be revived. Towers cover water worse than road, and the octopus can drown the hero, which is a defeat. Both water bosses are outside the waves: the built leviathan and octopus (`aquatic-design.md`) are wave members, here the scenario places them as scripted guards and set pieces.

**Why the rework.** The first version had Brenna fight the leviathan after she already held the relic, with no reason in the story; trust came only from holding the walls; and the Legion marched on the village though it wanted the relic. Now every fight is about the relic, trust is won or lost in one rescue, and the leviathan stands between Brenna and the sword.

**Why the map is shaped as it is** (`data/maps/drowned_abbey.json`; hand-edited from now on, see `.claude/rules/scenarios-and-maps.md` and the edit-handmade-map skill). The sea along the south, a harbour village with a quay, a church inside the walls, a broad river from the north edge to the sea beside the harbour so the octopus and leviathan can move between island and village. The island is reachable only from the village's bank over one **straight** bridge, and roads turn only on land: water monsters must be able to pass under bridges and fords predictably. The Legion comes down the village's bank, so towers by the village fight it on its way to the island; an east road crossing north of the island lets enemies come from the other side too. Harbour props are scenery kept free of buildings (`keep_free`). The broad sandy shore with sandbanks, reached through shallow water, gives the water side something to teach.

**The priests.** Two only: the rescue needs one to lose and one to stay and speak for the church either way. The eldest (named, her own look) must not die; the younger is the one caught, and losing him is no defeat. They live in the church and walk rounds outside (the eldest looks out over the river, the younger blesses the boats), as scenario 6's priest does.

**Early game.** Wave 1 waits until the player has built a watchtower by the shore (finished by the player, never prebuilt: the lesson is theirs), which teaches the water side first; the objective for it is gone once met. Pell's guild boats ferry in supplies after fixed waves, shown for real: a boat sails in, its goods go by cart to his stall (`ScenarioCaravan`), so arrival is visible.

**The seal and the relic.** The crypt starts sealed with a seal that has HP, like scenario 4's warehouse, so the Legion can break it. The relic is in one of five places (`mark["relic"]`: crypt, Lysa, ground, Brenna, home). Only Brenna carries it (dropped loot with an `"item"` is the hero's, never a gatherer's); she drops it wherever she is when switched out of Explore, and downed with it means lost anyway.

**The octopus rescue.** Between waves a priest walks to the quay; only when he arrives does the octopus leave the abbey and grab him. It holds him **unharmed while the alarm scene plays**, because the game runs on under a cutscene and towers would otherwise cut him free before the player can act (`Enemy.spared`, `OctopusBehavior.grab`). Then a countdown runs; he is tougher than a villager so the arm's damage does not preempt it. Towers and units go for a holding arm before anything else, even the octopus (`Scenario.arms_first`, `Enemy.holding`). Freed, he runs to the church, and only there is he rescued; caught again on the way, he is held again. After a rescue the octopus goes back to guard the abbey, so it is in Brenna's way later. If the octopus is already dead when the rescue is due, it counts as rescued. The outcome picks the branch:
- **Rescued:** the eldest comes to the gate and tells what the abbey hides (the Hand, why it was hidden, the vampires, something bigger in the deep). The saved priest stays in the church a good while before his rounds again.
- **Drowned:** the priests shut Brenna out. She never sets foot in the village, gates included (`Hero.barred`, `Pathing.find_path_outside`): a tent goes up outside the walls as in scenario 1 and 7, her home. Only she is shut out; the player gives orders as ever, and after one scene the priests say nothing. The dead priest's objective is crossed out in red (`"failed"`), and the scenario goes on.

**The Legion goes for the relic, not the huts.** A column led by Lysa goes wherever the relic is: the seal, the ground, or the church once it is home (breaking in like any enemy). It marches as a **company** in loose formation round her (`LegionCompany`), like an army, while the rest of a wave (monsters) goes for huts as usual. Its soldiers still fight what is in their way. `Enemy.errand` lets the company's object decide what an enemy does when a path ends, so the end of a path is not mistaken for a gate; while the errand holds the enemy, the siege leaves it alone.
- **Rescued:** one wave brings a Legion column; Lysa demands the relic and Brenna refuses her own captain. **When wave 6 is over the crypt unseals, if the relic is still sealed in it** (if the Legion broke the seal there is nothing to unseal). A wave only ends once its Legion column is beaten, so no separate "beaten" condition is needed for unsealing.
- **Drowned:** several waves bring a column, stronger each time; with the relic home, holding the village (the church is inside) is the point. After the last the priests give in, in this order: let her back in (the tent goes); then, if the crypt is still sealed, unseal it, or, if they hold the relic handed in at the gate, give it to her. The last two exclude each other. If the relic is elsewhere, they only let her in. Brenna brings a relic to the gate and goes back to her tent; the priests hold it but it is not hers until she has earned their trust.

**Lysa and the relic.** Breaking the seal wakes the leviathan, and Lysa searches the crypt for as long as Brenna's looting takes, ignoring it, then walks to the map edge with her soldiers. In the crypt and the church she must search; a relic on the ground she picks up at once, and while she fights it draws her. Reaching the edge with it loses. Beaten, a captain falls back (so scenario 10 stays as planned) and **drops the relic where she stands**; while falling back she picks nothing up. Her soldiers stay round it, so taking it back is hard and towers and barracks near the island pay off. She is always seen (`keep_in_sight`), and her wave's scene keeps the camera on her.

**The leviathan.** It sleeps until the crypt is first used, by Brenna entering the opened crypt or the Legion breaking the seal.
- Woken by **Brenna**: it rises, drives her off the island and blocks the crypt (`Treasure.blocked`) so the relic cannot be taken while it lives. **No Legion comes with it; only the normal waves go on.** This is intended design but not enforced in code, because it cannot happen: the crypt only opens after the Legion's waves are over, so Brenna cannot wake it while a column is still coming. Slain, Brenna returns for the relic.
- Woken by **the Legion** breaking the seal: Lysa takes the relic and flees; the leviathan need not be slain to bring the relic home.
- Its goals, moved between by the script through the deep water joining river and sea: guard the crypt while the relic is in it; sink Pell's boats (their goods are lost, so no supplies while it lives; a boat that arrives is lost whether or not it reached the pier); otherwise blast the shore like a wave leviathan. A boat beats the crypt, the crypt beats the shore. So it cannot be ignored for long.

**Winning** (`Scenario.can_win`, more than the shown objectives): relic home (and given to Brenna in the drowned branch) and the leviathan slain. If it is alive at that point the priests ask her to kill it, as a **choice**: yes shows the objective; no sends a messenger, who returns a wave later with a decree signed by Fiona asking the same. The choice is flavour: both end the same. Outro lines depend on the branch. The beat that follows: Lysa, herself for a moment, says the force at the abbey only meant to hold Brenna; the marshal Halvard already marches on her home village, and she and Pell must go back.

**Objectives, shown as they come:** the shore tower (start only); rescue the priest (optional, countdown); keep the relic from the Legion (optional, detail shows the seal and "Lysa has it"); bring the relic home (detail says where it is); slay the leviathan (shown once it blocks the crypt or after request or decree). Cards and hints say at each turn what changed (the island's ruins to restore for holding the abbey, Lysa with the relic, the relic on the ground, and so on).

**What it added to the engine, and why.** The church (`Church`, `"inside"` for a prebuilt building within the walls: the relic needs a home that can be defended); `LegionCompany` and `Enemy.errand`; seals with HP and `Treasure.blocked`; the octopus's scripted grab, `Enemy.spared`, `Scenario.arms_first`; `AquaBehavior.set_post` and queue entries' `"on_spawn"` and `"gap"`; `ScenarioRunner.hero_moves_out`, `Hero.barred`, `keep_in_sight`, `keep_free`; `Resident.home_tile` and `start_rounds`; `Scenario.rotate_kinds` / `rotate_count` so repeated waves grow by kinds in turn; objectives' `"failed"` and `"gone"`; a cutscene `"camera"` step that may be a Callable and a `"follow"` step.
