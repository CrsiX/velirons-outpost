# Story design

Status: **planned, nothing of it is built yet.** This page holds the story the game's scenarios, library texts and event log will draw on. It changes no rule or number. Anything marked *open* is not decided.

## 1. Premise

The kingdom of Veliron fell in a civil war. The old king died without a clear heir and two claimants fought for the crown. Each pulled the knights off the **Wardline** to fight the other, and the border was left empty. The wilderness that had always lived beyond the borders walked in, and the war brought worse things after it.

The elder claimant, a **woman** (*heir 1*, **Fiona**), is alive and now in **exile**. The younger, a **man** (*heir 2*, **Rhodri**), was gradually corrupted and is now **evil** (section 4).

The player is **neutral**. The outpost is a remote village that sided with neither claimant. It is the hero's home town, which is why it is remote and why it still stands.

## 2. The characters

Named so far: the hero (Brenna Hale), the merchant (Pell Dunmore), heir 1 (Fiona Aldmoor), the old king (Orsin Aldmoor), the old queen (Aislyn Aldmoor) and heir 2 (Rhodri Aldmoor). The royal family is **House Aldmoor**. Everyone else (villagers, units, traders) may have names for flavour, but nothing in the story depends on them. More named characters come later: the royal knights on the other side and the rulers of the other countries.

Text always refers to a person by first name. Last names show only in places like cutscene title cards, the library and journals. Other named characters will need last names when they are added.

**The hero** (**Brenna**) is a **woman**. One of the royal knights. She was in the military, so she served heir 2, who later became **evil**. She was **betrayed by that side** (how: *open*; likely she was among the troops sacrificed for the corruption's power, see section 4). She left and went back to her home town, the outpost. The game's hero (see `hero-design.md`) is this character. Her mode-based behaviour stays as it is; the story gives it a face. (The game's texts and `hero-design.md` still say "he" and would change with it.)

The military's distrust of heir 1 was about a woman **ruling and commanding the realm**, not about women serving in the ranks. The hero is a knight who earned her place, which makes her an exception worth a line of story: she served the side whose officers would not have accepted a woman as their queen.

**The merchant** (**Pell**) is a man. An old friend of the hero. When everything went wrong, he came back to the remote village, and that is why the outpost has a trader. He belongs to the **neutral guild** (section 6). He returns between scenarios.

The betrayal explains why the hero stays away from court, and the last scenarios carry a personal stake for her.

## 3. The Wardline

The kingdom's border defence: a chain of watchtowers manned by knights, paid for by the crown, so the wilderness never reached the farms. It is manpower and stone, not magic.

| Game element | Story meaning |
|---|---|
| Ruined watchtowers (claimed by the hero, restored by a builder) | Broken links of the Wardline. Restoring one rebuilds it. |
| Watchtowers, wall towers | Links that still stand or are rebuilt. |
| Light stones | Stay *light stones*: a building that helps the player, with no story role. |
| Walls and gates | The last intact stretch of the line. That enemies never get in is a game rule, not a spell. |
| Roads | The old King's Roads. Enemies walk them because they are the way in. |

## 4. How the kingdom fell

**The claimants**

- **The old king** is **Orsin**. His queen, **Aislyn**, died giving birth to heir 2. Both heirs are their children.
- **Heir 1** (**Fiona**, woman, the elder): supported by the **court mages and the church**. The old king never confirmed her as heir, because the military distrusted her ("a woman can't lead"). He waited, and waited too long: he died too early.
- **Heir 2** (**Rhodri**, man, the younger): supported by the **military**. He was not the rightful heir, but wanted the power. His greed is the root of his fall.

**The sequence**

1. The king dies without having confirmed an heir. The military backs heir 2, the court mages and the church back heir 1. The two sides are not aligned, and the war begins.
2. **Spies from the neighbouring countries** (below) offer heir 2 help, in some form, and that help slowly corrupts him. The story makes clear that the spies were the reason. It stays **unclear whom they belonged to**.
3. Both sides pull knights off the Wardline and the towers empty.
4. The wilderness walks in: goblins and orks first.
5. Renegades (deserters, unpaid soldiers, ruined merchants) prey on the roads.
6. Heir 2's corruption grows. To keep winning he sacrifices his own towns and soldiers for power, and that becomes part of the corruption. The hero is betrayed in this step.
7. The war's unburied dead rise. The evil is drawn by a kingdom full of death and gathers around heir 2. He does not create it: monsters still have no single source.
8. Heir 1 flees into exile, with what is left of the court mages and the church. The capital burns.

**The unlock sites**
- The **mage's tower** (apprentice) and the **ruined chapel** (acolyte) belong to heir 1's supporters, the court mages and the church. Unlocking a unit means finding what is left of them.
- The **stone circle** (summoner) belongs to **druids**. They stayed out of the question of the succession and kept their distance from the whole conflict. They are not part of heir 1's alliance.

**The neighbouring countries**

Veliron is a kingdom. Around it lie three rivals, each a different form of state:

| Country | Form of state | Names |
|---|---|---|
| A | A kingdom | Kingdom of Karmere |
| B | A republic | Republic of Lyrenne |
| C | A theocracy | The Holy Dominion of Aurelith |

Any of them, or several, could have sent the spies. The story never settles it. It may point at one in one scenario and at another in the next.

## 5. Monster categories

There is **no single source** of monsters. Categories exist because each arrived for its own reason, and the library can group by them. They are not people who were changed.

| Category | Members now | Planned or open | Why they came |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wilderness** | goblin, ork, rat, slime | spider (coming soon) | Always lived around the kingdom. The Wardline held them back. |
| **Renegades** (*Abtrünnige*) | thief | bandit, deserter, assassin | People who turned on their own side: the order collapsed. |
| **Undead** | skeleton | ghost, wraith, zombie | The war's dead, unburied. |
| **Evil** | vampire, necromancer | demon | Intelligent and malicious, drawn by the death. |
| **Arcane** (*open*) | witch, gargoyle | wisp, golem | Magic-made or magic-using. Goes with neither Wilderness nor Evil. |

Library and log text should use the category name ("a Renegade", "the Undead"), not a person's backstory.

## 6. Trade: the merchant guilds

A market that trades with the outside (planned) is how the story shows that the world beyond Veliron still exists.

- **The guild split.** The merchant guild tried to stay neutral in the war, but it was hit hard and broke apart. It split into several guilds that are now somewhat hostile to each other. Only a smaller version of the original remains: the **neutral guild**. The outpost and its merchant belong to it.
- **Why they deal with the outpost:** its walls make it the only safe stop on its stretch of road, it sits near the old kingdom's ruins where relics and salvage are worth coin, and the merchant is the hero's friend.
- **Enemies on the road:** Renegades raid caravans and steal gold on the same roads. The rival guilds are a second threat (they may hire Renegades: *open*).
- **Mechanics it could reuse:** the existing "buy material with gold" button is the first trade. The co-op caravan (a horse-drawn cart) can be the traders' arrival.
- **Growth:**

| Stage | What it is | Story reason |
|---|---|---|
| Peddler | A few goods in small amounts. | The merchant arrives alone, the first outsider in months. |
| Stall | More goods. | The merchant comes back. |
| Neutral guild market | Full trade, caravans. | The roads are safe enough. |

## 7. Scenarios: a campaign

The scenarios form one **campaign**: each builds on the last, and playing all of them tells the whole story. Each has its own settings (map, difficulty, size of the village) and explains why the monsters roam. The map generator is not part of the story. The hero and the merchant return in every scenario.

The detailed outline of every scenario (place, starting point, objective, problems, events, story, cutscenes) is in `scenarios-design.md`. The table below is the older, shorter draft. Where they differ, `scenarios-design.md` wins:

| # | Name | Story | New monsters | Market |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Homecoming | The hero wakes in a destroyed hut (opening cutscene). The village is ruined and the hero has to hold it. The war is a rumour. | Wilderness | None |
| 2 | The Old Friend | The merchant arrives, the first outsider in months, with news of the world and the guild. | Wilderness | Peddler, then a stall |
| 3 | The Wardline | Restore the broken watchtowers. Each makes the road safer. | Renegades | Neutral guild caravans |
| 4 | The Split | The rival guilds. Caravans are raided, and the rival guilds are hostile. The merchant tells how the guild broke apart. | Renegades (more) | Full, with a rival threat |
| 5 | The Betrayal | The hero's old company. Journals and relics tell what really happened to her. | Arcane (*open*) | Full |
| 6 | The Dead | The war's battlefields. Corpses matter more because they get raised. | Undead | Full |
| 7 | The Exile | Traces of the fled claimant: a messenger, a camp, a request. Help or refuse. | Wilderness and Undead | Full |
| 8 | The Pale Court | The evil claimant's court appears. | Evil | Full |
| 9 | The Last Wardline | The enemy host comes. The 25-wave mid-bosses (roadmap) belong here. | All | Full |
| 10 | The Crown | The ending. The choice of what to do with the crown and the exile (*open*). | All | Full |

**Nothing carries over between scenarios** (no units, relics, hero levels or market stage). Every scenario starts from its own scripted baseline, so any one of them can be replayed on its own. The story carries over, not the game state.

**The hero's death.** In scenarios 1 to 3, at the home village, she revives as in the normal game (her people bring her back). In **all other scenarios her being downed is a defeat**, and the player can try again. This includes scenario 9, although it is at home: there is no one left to bring her back. Away from home nobody can bring her back. This is a per-scenario setting (to go into `config.gd` when built), so a scenario can change it, and a message on entering the first away scenario should say so.

Each scenario opens and closes with a cutscene (section 8). The outro plays when the scenario's **objective** is fulfilled, so every scenario needs one (*open*: what each objective is; the table above names the story, not the win condition).

*Open:* whether scenario 1 doubles as the tutorial.

## 8. Where the story shows

The story is told through **two cutscenes per scenario** and through places the game already has (the library, treasures, the log and the title screen).

- **Intro cutscene (every scenario):** plays at the start and introduces the part of the story the scenario covers. The one for scenario 1 has the hero wake in a destroyed hut and look around the village (exact content *open*).
- **Outro cutscene (every scenario):** plays when the scenario's objective is fulfilled and gives further information that closes the scenario and leads to the next. The campaign's last one is the story's ending (*open*).
- **Format of the cutscenes:** *open* (for example still pictures with text, or a short scene in the game's engine). Whether they can be skipped, and whether they can be seen again from the title screen or the library, is also *open*.
- **Title screen:** a one-line intro (draft below).
- **Library:** category groupings on the Enemies tab, a short lore line per entry, lore on the Places tab for unlock sites and ruined towers.
- **Treasures and relics:** journal fragments from the war, a named piece of regalia as a relic (the roadmap wants usable relics).
- **Event log:** a line when a Wardline tower is restored, the first time a category appears, the merchant's arrival.
- **Tutorial:** unchanged, or one line of framing at its start.

Draft intro:

> *Veliron fell in a winter of civil war. The towers stand empty and the roads lead only to your gates. One outpost kept its post. Nine huts, four towers, and a steward who sided with no one. Hold the line.*
